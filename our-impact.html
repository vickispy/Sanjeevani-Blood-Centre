Understanding The Need

The Lifelong Journey of Transfusion-Dependent Patients

Genetic hemoglobin disorders require ongoing clinical vigilance, fresh compatible blood, and compassionate community solidarity.

Thalassemia Major

Thalassemia is an inherited blood disorder where the body cannot produce adequate normal hemoglobin. Children with Thalassemia Major often require blood transfusions every 2 to 4 weeks starting from infancy to survive and maintain normal growth.

Providing these units free of cost significantly reduces catastrophic financial toxicity for struggling families while ensuring patients receive fresh, screened PRBC units.

Sickle Cell Anaemia

Sickle Cell Anaemia causes red blood cells to distort into rigid, crescent shapes, obstructing microvascular circulation and triggering agonizing pain crises, stroke risk, and organ damage.

Regular or acute exchange transfusions replenish healthy round red cells, alleviate ischemic pain, and protect vital organs.
